# Consoles: what the market calls old, and what it calls collectible

Pulled 2026-09-29, because the desk has exactly one console row — "current
gen", $225 — and a PS4, an N64 and a Dreamcast all land on it. Three
different markets, one number.

## Where the line is

**Roughly 2001.** Machines made before it have stopped falling and started
rising; machines made after it are used appliances still on the way down.
It is not nostalgia alone - it is the media. Cartridge and early-optical
machines were not made in the hundreds of millions, they are no longer
manufactured, and the things that play those games are finite. A PS4 is
not finite.

The boundary sits on the 2000-2001 machines - PS2, GameCube, original
Xbox - which have all crossed over inside the last few years.

## THE TRAP, AND IT IS THE WHOLE STORY

**Once a machine is in the collector band, the variant is worth more than
the machine.** Same box, different sticker, 3x to 50x:

- PS3 60GB backward-compatible **$206** against PS3 160GB **$75**. Same
  shelf. Nearly 3x, and it is a spec you cannot see from across a counter.
- PS3 20GB **$269**, also on that shelf.
- Wii blue **$121** against Wii white **$50**.
- Xbox 360 Slim 250GB **$117** against 360 Core **$52**.
- GameCube, Heineken promo: loose **$2,499**.
- N64 limited colourways, runs of 10k-50k: **$6,400-$7,300** graded.
- Game Boy Advance SP, Pikachu and Pokemon Center editions: **$1,000-$4,500** CIB.
- DSi Pokemon White: loose **$279**, CIB **$1,200+**.
- PSP Spider-Man 2 bundle: loose **$328**.

A flat book row prices every one of these identically and every one of
them wrong. The row is not the answer for this band - the row is what
tells the counter to go look it up.

## How good is this

**Middling, and one-sourced.** Every figure above is PriceCharting, which
is real completed eBay sales rather than asks - that part is solid, and it
is the standard reference the trade uses.

What is wrong with it:

- **It runs high on consoles specifically.** PriceCharting's own
  methodology excludes shipping, but it does so inconsistently: it ignores
  shipping on listings that charge it and does not subtract it from ones
  that bake it in. On a heavy boxed console that gap is real money, and
  independent write-ups put the variation at up to $100 on large lots. So
  read these as ceilings.
- **Some rows are thin.** Saturn at 4 listings, Dreamcast at 14, Wii U at
  one number. A "sealed" figure resting on 2 sales a year is a curiosity,
  not a price.
- **Region contamination.** The PS4 and PSP rows came back PAL, the Vita
  rows came back Japan-only. PAL and JP prices are not this counter's
  prices. The PS4 and Vita numbers here are the weakest on the page - the
  PS4 figures above are street/buyback data instead, and there is no clean
  US Vita figure at all.
- **It is one source.** The 46/46 cross-check that was run against
  GunBroker has no equivalent here. Nothing above has been checked against
  a second set of sold prices.

These are resale figures - what one SELLS for. They are not what the shop
pays. The category rate still does that work.
