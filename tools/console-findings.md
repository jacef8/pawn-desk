# Consoles: what the market calls old, and what it calls collectible

Pulled 2026-09-29, because the desk has exactly one console row — "current
gen", $225 — and a PS4, an N64 and a Dreamcast all land on it. Three
different markets, one number.

## Where the line is

**Roughly 2001.** Machines made before it have stopped falling and started
rising; machines made after it are used appliances still on the way down.
It is not nostalgia alone - it is the media. Cartridge and early-optical
machines were not made in the hundreds of millions, they are no longer
manufactured, and the things that play those games are finite. A PS4 is
not finite.

The boundary sits on the 2000-2001 machines - PS2, GameCube, original
Xbox - which have all crossed over inside the last few years.

## THE TRAP, AND IT IS THE WHOLE STORY

**Once a machine is in the collector band, the variant is worth more than
the machine.** Same box, different sticker, 3x to 50x:

- PS3 60GB backward-compatible **$206** against PS3 160GB **$75**. Same
  shelf. Nearly 3x, and it is a spec you cannot see from across a counter.
- PS3 20GB **$269**, also on that shelf.
- Wii blue **$121** against Wii white **$50**.
- Xbox 360 Slim 250GB **$117** against 360 Core **$52**.
- GameCube, Heineken promo: loose **$2,499**.
- N64 limited colourways, runs of 10k-50k: **$6,400-$7,300** graded.
- Game Boy Advance SP, Pikachu and Pokemon Center editions: **$1,000-$4,500** CIB.
- DSi Pokemon White: loose **$279**, CIB **$1,200+**.
- PSP Spider-Man 2 bundle: loose **$328**.

A flat book row prices every one of these identically and every one of
them wrong. The row is not the answer for this band - the row is what
tells the counter to go look it up.

## How good is this

**Middling, and one-sourced.** Every figure above is PriceCharting, which
is real completed eBay sales rather than asks - that part is solid, and it
is the standard reference the trade uses.

What is wrong with it:

- **It runs high on consoles specifically.** PriceCharting's own
  methodology excludes shipping, but it does so inconsistently: it ignores
  shipping on listings that charge it and does not subtract it from ones
  that bake it in. On a heavy boxed console that gap is real money, and
  independent write-ups put the variation at up to $100 on large lots. So
  read these as ceilings.
- **Some rows are thin.** Saturn at 4 listings, Dreamcast at 14, Wii U at
  one number. A "sealed" figure resting on 2 sales a year is a curiosity,
  not a price.
- **Region contamination.** The PS4 and PSP rows came back PAL, the Vita
  rows came back Japan-only. PAL and JP prices are not this counter's
  prices. The PS4 and Vita numbers here are the weakest on the page - the
  PS4 figures above are street/buyback data instead, and there is no clean
  US Vita figure at all.
- **It is one source.** The 46/46 cross-check that was run against
  GunBroker has no equivalent here. Nothing above has been checked against
  a second set of sold prices.

These are resale figures - what one SELLS for. They are not what the shop
pays. The category rate still does that work.

# The cross-check, 29 Sep

The section above was one-sourced and said so. This is the second source.

## Racketboy's hardware price guide

An enthusiast reference built the opposite way round from PriceCharting -
by hand, from what the community sees, rather than scraped off completed
listings. Its loose bands against PriceCharting's single figure:

    NES       $50-$160     PriceCharting $94
    SNES      $50-$225     PriceCharting $110
    Genesis   $40-$160     PriceCharting $75

**PriceCharting sits mid-range of the community guide every time.** Two
sources built by different methods, agreeing. That is the cross-check the
gun data had (46/46 against GunBroker) and the console data did not.

It also explains the SPREAD, which the single figure hid. Racketboy states
what its ends mean: the LOW is "console hardware only, no packaging,
minimal accessories like a power adapter", working; the HIGH includes "a
controller or two, power and video cables, and excellent cosmetic
condition". So the 3x range is completeness AND cosmetics together - not
completeness alone, which is the mistake it would be easy to make here.
The desk asks about condition separately, and pricing the scratches twice
is a bug this codebase has already shipped once.

Caveat: Racketboy's figures were last updated November 2023, and retro has
risen about 15% a year since across two sources. Its RATIOS are what was
used, not its levels.

## CIB premium, second source

Two trade write-ups put a CIB game at 2x-5x a loose one, or "40-80% more"
more conservatively. Measured loose->CIB on CONSOLES is 2.8x median
(SNES 3.5x, N64 3.4x, Saturn 3.2x, Game Boy 3.0x, NES 2.8x, PS2 2.6x,
Xbox 2.3x, GameCube 2.1x; Dreamcast 1.4x and PS1 1.3x are the exceptions).
Consistent with the upper half of the independent range.

## What a dealer actually pays

The figures in this file are RESALE. Two pawn-trade sources put a shop's
offer at 25-60% of resale, and one names an Xbox 360 at $10-30 at the
counter against a $52-117 resale - which is 20-40%, and consistent. The
category rate already does this conversion; nothing here changes it.

eStarland's and DKOldies' own buy prices would have been the best source
of all for this - a dealer's posted cash offer is exactly the number Jace
needs - and both return 403 to an automated fetch. Not pursued further:
their terms are the same line WorthPoint sits on, and a person clicking
through is fine where a program is not.

## What is still one-sourced

The absolute price levels for Saturn, Dreamcast, GameCube, PS2, original
Xbox, PS1 and every handheld. Racketboy covers NES, SNES and Genesis only,
so those three are the only rows with two independent price sources behind
them. The rest rest on PriceCharting alone, with the shipping bias above.

No US PlayStation Vita figure. Neo Geo, TurboGrafx-16, Atari, Intellivision
and ColecoVision remain unmeasured and have no row.
